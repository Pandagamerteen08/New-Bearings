# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

New Bearings is intended to support learning, experimentation, and constructive
collaboration.

We want contributors and community members to be treated with respect,
regardless of their experience level, background, identity, or preferred way
of learning.

Participation in the project should not require someone to tolerate harassment,
humiliation, discrimination, or intimidation.

## Expected behavior

Participants are expected to:

- Treat others respectfully.
- Discuss technical disagreements constructively.
- Criticize ideas and implementations rather than attacking people.
- Give useful feedback without humiliating others.
- Respect different levels of experience.
- Be patient with beginners.
- Acknowledge mistakes and correct them where appropriate.
- Respect privacy and avoid sharing another person's information without
  permission.
- Follow applicable project rules and platform requirements.
- Help keep technical discussions focused and useful.

## Unacceptable behavior

Examples of unacceptable conduct include:

- Harassment, threats, or targeted intimidation.
- Discriminatory or demeaning personal attacks.
- Repeated unwanted contact after a request to stop.
- Publishing private information without authorization.
- Deliberately disrupting project discussions or collaboration.
- Impersonation intended to deceive or harm others.
- Sharing malicious files, credentials, or harmful links.
- Retaliating against someone for raising a concern in good faith.
- Deliberately misrepresenting another contributor's work.

This list provides examples rather than an exhaustive catalog.

## Technical disagreement

Disagreement about architecture, programming languages, interface design,
accessibility, or project priorities is normal.

Contributors should explain their reasoning, identify trade-offs, and remain
open to evidence.

A rejected suggestion is not automatically disrespectful treatment. Likewise,
a technically correct argument does not excuse personal abuse.

## Reporting a concern

Report conduct concerns through an appropriate project-maintainer contact or
the project's available reporting channel.

Include enough context to explain what happened. Share private messages or
other sensitive evidence only when necessary and with appropriate care.

Do not publish confidential details in a public issue merely to make a report
visible.

If a report concerns a security vulnerability, use the process described in
SECURITY.md instead of disclosing exploit details publicly.

## Review and enforcement

Project maintainers may review reports, ask for relevant context, and take
reasonable action to protect participants and the project.

Depending on the circumstances, action may include:

- A request to correct behavior.
- A warning.
- Restrictions on participation.
- Removal of harmful material where the platform permits it.
- Temporary or permanent exclusion from project spaces.

Responses should consider the severity, context, available evidence, and risk
of further harm.

The project should not promise absolute confidentiality when a report may need
to be shared with people responsible for addressing it.

## Good-faith reporting

Reports should be made honestly and should not knowingly fabricate evidence.

A report does not need to prove every detail before it can be considered.
Retaliation against someone for raising a concern in good faith is unacceptable.

## Scope

This code applies to participation in project-managed spaces and to conduct
that materially affects project collaboration.

Third-party platforms may have additional rules. Participants are responsible
for following those rules as well.

## A living document

Maintainers may revise this code as the project and its community develop.
Substantive changes should be documented clearly.
